What we do

  1. 01Rung 1, Who has it now

    Market

    Agencies call it: strategy, ICP, market research

    • The segment, named narrowly enough to list.
    • The trigger: who has the problem this month, with the public evidence.
    • The exclusions: who looks right and is not, and why.
    • The buying committee: who signs, who uses it, who can block it.
    • The alternatives: what they use today instead of you.
    • Pricing and packaging: how to put the first price together, what goes in the box, and what stays out. You set the number, and we never suggest one.
    • Channels: which one to open first, and which to leave for later, with the reason.

    In your repository: market/

  2. 02Rung 1 into rung 2

    Message

    Agencies call it: positioning and messaging

    • The positioning conclusion, derived from who has the problem rather than written ahead of it.
    • One message per role in the buying committee.
    • Every claim in your messages checked against something you can prove, in a claims file you keep.
    • LinkedIn notes and email sequences, each line traceable to its source quote.
    • An elevator pitch: the two sentences you say when someone asks what you do.

    In your repository: message/

  3. 03Rung 2, Twenty conversations

    Sending

    Agencies call it: outbound, SDR as a service

    • Twenty named people, each with the quoted line that put them on the list.
    • We prepare every message. You press Send, from your own account and your own domain.
    • Replies worked together, up to the pilot ask.
    • A weekly report: replies, qualified conversations, the reason for every no.
    • One change tested at a time, written down before it is read.
    • Sending setup on your own accounts: domain records, mailbox, warm-up plan, LinkedIn profile. You hold every account and every password.

    In your repository: sending/, experiments/

  4. 04Included in the eight weeks

    Founder profile and content

    Agencies call it: founder-led content

    • Your LinkedIn profile rewritten for the market chosen, so the person who gets your note finds the same sentence on your profile.
    • Twelve post topics taken from your own material, four of them drafted. You post them.

    In your repository: profile/

  5. 05Included in the eight weeks

    Sales kit

    Agencies call it: sales enablement

    • A discovery script: the questions that establish whether they have the problem now, and who signs.
    • A written definition of a qualified conversation.
    • An objection library built from the real noes, each with its source.
    • One card per alternative they use today.
    • A pilot proposal template: two weeks, fixed scope, and you set the price.
    • A demo script: what to show, in what order, and what to leave out.
    • An ROI calculator your buyer can open. It ships blank. You put your own numbers in, and any number left in it names where it came from.
    • One-pager ($250 founding rate, $500 list) and pitch deck of up to 12 slides ($1,000 founding rate, $2,500 list) as separate fixed-price lines.

    In your repository: kit/

  6. 06Included in the eight weeks

    Pipeline and reporting

    Agencies call it: CRM, RevOps

    • The pipeline is one file: stage, date, next step, reason lost.
    • Definitions written before the first send: reply, qualified conversation, pilot proposed, pilot paid.
    • Kill conditions written before the first send: the numbers that say this market says no.
    • CRM setup in HubSpot or Pipedrive when the file stops being enough: pipeline stages, email and calendar connected, two or three automations, one training session. You create our seat in your own portal with the narrowest role that can build a pipeline, nothing of your customers' data is copied to our machines, and you remove the seat at handover. $1,000 founding rate, $2,000 list, as a separate fixed-price line.

    In your repository: pipeline/

  7. 07Included in the eight weeks, when there is a customer to write about

    Proof

    Agencies call it: case studies

    • Your first case study, written with your customer's permission and only when there is a customer to write about: the situation, what was done, the numbers, and what did not work.
    • A permission note your customer signs before anything is published.

    The one case I can show is my own, from before Vurell →

    In your repository: proof/

What we do not do, and why

  • Paid ads.

    Ads scale a message that is already proven. Before the first customers nothing is proven yet.

  • SEO and content programmes.

    Not in scope. Nothing here is built to pay off over quarters, and the engagement runs eight weeks.

  • Cold calling and appointment setting.

    We do not sell meetings by the piece, and nobody phones in your name.

  • Sending in your name without you.

    Every message goes from your account after you have read it.

  • Lists by the thousand.

    A search gives us candidates. We read them one at a time and deliver twenty, each with the quoted line that put it there, and the list of everyone rejected with the reason.

  • Retainers and guarantees.

    Fixed scope, public price; no meetings, customers or revenue are promised, and we say so before you pay.
