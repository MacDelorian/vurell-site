The free message check

Would it get read?

Paste a cold message. You get a verdict per rule, the words that triggered it, and what to change first.

It runs inside this page. Nothing you paste leaves your browser: no server, no request, nothing stored, no email asked. English-language messages only.

How to read it

Each rule says pass, fail or not applicable, shows the words that decided it, and gives its reason.

Looks like in a verdict means the rule is guessing: the words are shown so you can judge. Every rule has a weight, and the heaviest one that fails is what to change first.

What to change first

Your message as it was read. Words that fail a rule are marked, and the small label is the rule.

    What this checks

    Form, and the habits that get a message ignored: how long it is, who the first sentence is about, how many things it asks for and how big they are, links, claims with nothing behind them, template fields that did not fill, the subject line.

    Every verdict shows the words that triggered it. The rules are written to miss a fault sooner than accuse you of one that is not there.

    It reads English only. A message in another language will pass rules it should fail.

    What it cannot

    It cannot know whether the person has the problem, or whether they can sign. A message can pass every rule here and still go to someone who will never buy. That is decided by the list, before the first word is written.

    It cannot tell whether what you say about the reader is true either. It only sees that something is there.

    Who could sign counts the signers among your last twenty recipients. Services covers the rest: who to sell to, what to say to them, and the sending.

    The rules, and why each one is a rule

    Every rule runs on every message. The weight decides only which failing rule is named first. None of this is about timing or tricks: it is what a stranger sees in the first look.
