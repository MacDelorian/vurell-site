An engagement, step by step

Nothing below happened. The company is invented, the people are roles rather than names, and the numbers were chosen to show what each step produces.

  1. 01

    Market

    The company

    Fernbrook Ledger, a seed-stage B2B SaaS that reconciles freight invoices against delivery records. Two engineers, no salesperson, a product that works.

    The market

    Freight brokerages in the UK and Ireland between ten and sixty people. At that size the person who feels the problem is the person who signs. Carriers and anything over two hundred people were left out, because there the signature is three seats away.

    Illustrative example. Not a client. The figures show the shape of the work, not results we have delivered.

    The read

    Forty-six people read one at a time from what they leave in public. Twenty kept. Twenty-six killed, and each kill is written down with the reason: nine where the title spends a budget somebody above it owns, seven where a sales seat at the same company already owns the buy, six with nothing public in ninety days showing the problem now, four over two hundred people.

  2. 02

    Message

    Three of the twenty kept, and the line that kept them. A managing director who wrote publicly about chasing a credit note for six weeks. An operations director whose company posted a job for a reconciliation clerk. A finance lead who asked a public forum how other brokers close a month with paperwork missing.

  3. 03

    Sending

    The sending

    Twenty notes, one per name, each opening on the line that put the name on the list. The founder pressed Send from their own account, twenty over four weeks.

  4. 04

    First customers

    The replies

    Four wrote back. Two said not now and gave a reason, which went into the repository. Two kept talking, and one of those two reached the point where a pilot could be proposed.

    The pilot

    Two weeks, a fixed sum, one month of that broker's invoices reconciled by hand before anybody writes software. One page, sent by the founder.

What the founder keeps

Forty-six judgements, twenty names with their quoted evidence, twenty-six kills with the reason each, every message, every reply, and the rules that decided them. Plain text, in a repository the founder owns.

We publish no reply rate, and the numbers above are not one. They were chosen to show the shape of a week's work.

Who it is for

This is for founders who shipped something good and have never sold anything.
